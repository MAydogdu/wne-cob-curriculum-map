National Benchmark of 90-Credit & Reduced-Credit Undergraduate Degrees

A comparative survey of accredited institutions developing or conferring 90-to-96 credit bachelor's degrees. Analyzed across discipline offerings, formal degree nomenclature, curricular architecture (Core vs. Gen Ed vs. Major), and regional accreditation standards (including NECHE, NWCCU, and HLC).

🏷️ Degree Naming (Accreditor Rule)

NECHE and state regulators (e.g. Mass BHE) mandate that 90-credit degrees be distinguished with an explicit prefix (e.g. "Applied Bachelor's" or BAS), reserving traditional BA/BS designations for 120-credit programs.

✂️ The 30-Credit Reduction Formula

Across all benchmarked models, the 30-credit cut is achieved by slashing free open electives (down to 0–6 cr) and streamlining Gen Ed (down to 24–31 cr), while 100% of major/core rigor is preserved.

🎯 Target Disciplines

Concentrated in applied, high-demand professions: Business Administration, Healthcare Management, Cybersecurity, IT, Applied Psychology, and Communications. Licensure fields with statutory 150/120 cr mandates (CPA, ABET Engineering) are avoided.

🛡️ Consumer Transparency & Bridge

NECHE requires institutions to disclose that some graduate programs or employers may restrict sub-120 credit degrees, and requires a guaranteed articulation bridge to seamlessly complete a full 120-credit degree.
